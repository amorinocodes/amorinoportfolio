The Problem

Young travellers want real culture.
They keep finding tourist traps.

58% of Australians aged 18 to 25 were planning overseas trips in 2024, with Asia-Pacific as their top destination. They want something genuine — local food stalls, language lessons, real conversations. But without the right tools, they end up on the same guided bus tours as everyone else.

64%
of young travellers knew about Smartraveller — but only 37% actually reviewed destination-specific advice before travelling
+38%
increase in Australian visits to Japan, alongside surges to China (+35%) and Vietnam (+26%). Asia travel is booming.
40%

"Young Australians seek authentic, culturally rich experiences, yet face barriers in awareness, planning, and cultural understanding. Without effective models, many are funnelled into tourist traps — leading to diluted experiences for them and missed economic opportunities for local communities."

The Solution

Roamy — like having a friend in town who knows where to go

Roamy connects travellers directly with verified locals who host experiences — a Thai tea ceremony, a cooking class from someone's grandmother's kitchen, a street food walk through neighbourhoods no tourist map shows you.

It creates mutual value: travellers get genuine cultural access, locals earn income and get recognised for sharing what they know best.

How We Got There

Three rounds of testing,
each one sharper than the last

We didn't design in a vacuum. Every decision came from something we heard, observed, or tested — starting from a pantomime in a classroom and ending at a polished, animated prototype.

01
Concept Testing
Pantomime and Low-Fidelity
We simulated a Thai café cultural exchange with 7 participants. Before touching a screen, we put people inside the experience itself — measuring whether the concept resonated emotionally before committing to any interface decisions.
Think-aloud method Likert-scale pre/post survey Sketched booking wireframe
02
Structural Design
Mid-Fidelity Prototype
Moved into Figma with consistent components, real imagery, and colour. Drew from Airbnb's booking flow, Headspace's emotional tone, and Booking.com's navigation clarity to shape familiar but fresh patterns.
Simplified ticket screen Trust indicators added Navigation overhauled
03
Final Polish
High-Fidelity and Brand
Finalised the logo — globe embedded in the O — the colour system, onboarding animation, and resolved all UI inconsistencies. The brand had to feel playful and modern, youthful without being childish.
Onboarding animation Brand identity locked Figma prototype final

Key Insights from Testing

On Trust
Verified badges increased user confidence — but without explanation, people didn't know what they meant. We added context to every trust signal.
On Navigation
Users wanted clearer transitions between browsing, booking, and managing. We rebuilt the flow so each state felt distinct and purposeful.
On Cultural Framing
People engaged more with activities described as cultural exchange rather than tours. The language we used mattered as much as the features themselves.
On the Ticket Screen
The original check-in flow was too cluttered. Stripping it to a QR code and essential info dramatically improved confidence and reduced confusion during testing.

Key Features

What Roamy actually does

01
Explore
Browse local experiences organised by duration and category. Personalised recommendations surface activities that match your interests and travel style.
02
Manage
Track bookings and itineraries. Coordinate solo or group plans, adjust on the go, and never lose track of what is coming up next.
03
Experience Detail
Each listing has full host profiles, Roamy Local Badges, and honest reviews. Enough to build genuine trust before you even show up.
04
QR Ticket
A digital ticket for every booking. Scan to check in, verify the host, and get a layer of safety built directly into the flow.
05
Host Side
Locals create profiles, design experiences, and get notified when someone books. Running a cultural experience should be simple, not a second job.
06
Profile and Badges
Achievements, cultural milestones, and a network of fellow Roamers. Travel becomes something you build on, not just consume.

My Role

UX Designer and Ideation Lead

Led ideation and concept generation — shaping the research direction and making sure the human stayed at the centre of every decision we made

Built the wireframes and UI — from low-fidelity sketches through to high-fidelity Figma screens, leading on the technical and visual build of the product

Ran user testing sessions — facilitated the pantomime, led survey analysis, and documented feedback across all three rounds of testing

Guided participants personally — because this topic was personal to me, I wasn't just a designer running a test. I had a stake in what we were creating.

Balanced creativity with structure — learned through this project how design gets stronger when you build collaboratively, not in isolation

Honest Reflection
What Worked
Strong team collaboration with clear roles kept momentum steady throughout. The user testing fair was one of the most energising parts — watching real people engage with something we made from scratch confirmed we were solving the right problem.
What I Would Do Differently
I would push harder on the local perspective — our design skewed traveller-first. I would also test more scenarios earlier and think more seriously about business viability from week one, not just as an afterthought.
What It Taught Me
That design can shape feelings as much as functionality. Iteration is not failure — it is the process. And the work gets better when you genuinely care about the problem you are solving, not just the solution you are presenting.
